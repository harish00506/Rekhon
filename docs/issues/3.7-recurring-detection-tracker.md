## What was built

Five slices, in order:

1. **The rulebook row.** `RULE-RECUR-DETECT` in `ai/rules/rules-kb.json` (domain `saving`,
   `formula_id: recurring_series_match`, v1.0) with `min_occurrences: 2`, `amount_tolerance_pct: 5`
   and `cadence_tolerance_days: {weekly: 2, monthly: 4, yearly: 10}`, mirrored in `rulebook.md`.
   Mirrored into Kotlin as an injected `RecurringRules` per **ADR-0005**, guarded by a new
   `RulebookDriftTest`.
2. **Schema v9 → v10.** `recurring_rule.dismissed_at_utc_millis` — one nullable `ADD COLUMN`, no
   backfill (null already means "not dismissed" for every existing row). `observeForProfile` gained
   `AND dismissed_at_utc_millis IS NULL`; `observeDecidedNames` and `dismiss` are new.
3. **`:domain:engines:recurring`.** `RecurringEngine.detect(input): Result<List<RecurringSeries>, AppError>`
   — group by normalised merchant, classify the median gap, verify every gap and every amount,
   project the next due date with `java.time`. `ENGINE.md` written from the template.
4. **The repository seam.** `RecurringRepository` (`observeSuggestions`, `confirm`, `dismiss`) plus
   `TransactionDao.observeRecurringCandidates` — a four-column projection over a two-year window.
5. **The transactions-list section.** `TransactionsRecurringUi.kt`, a `RecurringEvent` sealed
   interface, and a card that shows the occurrence *dates* as its evidence.

## Decisions worth remembering

- **Classify on the median gap, then verify every gap.** The median (or mean) alone accepts
  1 Mar / 31 Mar / 30 Apr / 30 Jun as "monthly". The `all` check is one extra line and is the
  difference between a detector and a coincidence generator. Pinned by a fixture whose median is a
  clean 30 and whose last gap is 61 days.
- **A dismissal is not a tombstone.** `deleted_at_utc_millis` means the rule is gone and every read
  filters it out — which would let the detector re-propose the merchant. `dismissed_at_utc_millis` is
  a separate column, and `observeDecidedNames` reads rows with **no filter at all**: confirmed,
  pending, dismissed and tombstoned merchants have each been shown to the user once already.
- **Rule ids are derived, not minted** (`<profile>:recurring:<merchant>`) — the mechanism
  `netWorthSnapshotId` already uses. It makes the write idempotent under `REPLACE` and removed an
  `IdGenerator` from the repository: a derived key is reproducible in a test by construction (P-08).
- **`occurrences` carries dates, not just ids.** The first cut returned `List<String>`; the card
  needs the dates for P-02, and re-attaching them in the repository would have meant a parallel DTO.
  Changing the engine's type was the smaller diff and the better shape.
- **The suggestions flow is collected separately from the screen's main `combine`.** A proposal is
  optional: if its query fails, the section renders nothing and the ledger above it is untouched
  (P-04). A sixth source inside that `combine` would take the whole screen down with it.
- **The lower median is signed.** For an even count of *outflows* it picks the larger expense. Which
  of the two middle values is "the" median is arbitrary either way; P-08 requires only that it be
  fixed, so a test pins it rather than leaving it to be discovered.
- **`Cadence.periodDays` (7/30/365) is a match target only.** Projecting with `+30 days` would put
  the next monthly bill on 2 March after 31 January; `advance()` uses `plusMonths`/`plusYears`.

## Gates proven to fail before being trusted

The governance audit's lesson (`docs/report/2026-07-25-governance-standards-audit.md`) is that a gate
nobody has watched go red is not a gate. Two were checked here:

- **`RulebookDriftTest`** — `amount_tolerance_pct` was edited from 5 to 7 in `ai/rules/rules-kb.json`
  and `:domain:engines:recurring:test` went **red** on `the match thresholds match RULE-RECUR-DETECT`.
  The rulebook was then restored and the suite re-run green.
- **The dismissal exclusion** — proven on the device, not only in a test: a proposal was rejected,
  the screen was left and re-opened, and it did not come back.
